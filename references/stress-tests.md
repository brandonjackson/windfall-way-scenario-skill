# Scenario Stress Tests

Structured questions to apply to a draft scenario before tuning.
Work through these after drafting (Stage 5) and use the results to
inform the tuning pass (Stage 6).

---

## Internal Consistency

- Map the key economic arguments in the scenario. Are they all
  internally consistent? Does A actually lead to B?
- Are there any causal claims that contradict each other?
- Do the numbers add up? (e.g. if unemployment is X%, do the fiscal
  implications match?)
- Are there implicit assumptions that haven't been stated? Could they
  undermine the logic if surfaced?

## Audience Resonance

- Would this scenario resonate with the specific audience in the brief?
- Does it speak in their institutional language?
- Does it connect to decisions they actually face, or does it float
  above their responsibilities?
- Is there a risk that participants dismiss it as irrelevant to their
  context?

## Plausibility

- Where is the scenario most vulnerable to "that would never happen"
  objections?
- Are those vulnerabilities fatal (would derail the workshop) or
  acceptable (can be acknowledged and moved past)?
- Are the timelines credible? Could the changes described plausibly
  unfold in the time given?
- Does the scenario rely on any single point of failure that a
  sceptic would immediately target?

## Productive Tension

- What is the central dilemma? Can you state it in one sentence?
- Does the scenario force a genuine trade-off, or does it have an
  obvious "right answer"?
- Are there at least two defensible positions a participant could
  take in response?
- Does the ending hand the dilemma to the room, or does it resolve
  prematurely?

## Emotional Register

- Does the scenario land emotionally or does it read as abstract?
- Is there at least one moment that connects macro dynamics to lived
  experience?
- Is the tone appropriate for the audience? (Too provocative risks
  defensiveness; too measured risks boredom.)

## Coverage and Gaps

- What dimensions of the world does the scenario cover? What does it
  leave out?
- Are the omissions deliberate (keeping it focused) or accidental
  (blind spots)?
- Does the scenario over-index on one causal mechanism at the expense
  of others?

## Workshop Dynamics

- What is the most likely unproductive conversation this scenario
  will trigger?
- Are there any terms, claims, or framings that will distract from
  the core dilemma?
- Is the scenario short enough to read aloud and absorb in the
  workshop setting?
- Does it give participants enough to work with, without
  over-specifying their response?
