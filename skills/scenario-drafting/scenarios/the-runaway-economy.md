# The Runaway Economy

> Scenario 2 from the Windfall Trust "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI" workshop, September 2025, San Francisco. Created by Anna Yelizarova, Danny Buerkli, Jamais Cascio, and David Evan Harris.

AI agents replace human workers across many sectors with minimal regulatory constraints, creating massive productivity gains that accrue to capital owners while many people become economically excluded.

## State of AI Tech

General-purpose AI systems are capable of fully automating most cognitive and creative tasks. Breakthroughs in architecture and abundant energy unlock rapid scaling. Firms move quickly to replace human labor across white-collar and service industries. Firms that embrace full automation surge ahead. "AI-first" companies (lean, hyper-productive, and minimally staffed) rapidly outcompete large incumbents that attempt to remain human-first, retaining traditional workforces for ethical or reputational reasons. Within a few years, refusing to automate becomes economically unsustainable, and human-first firms either collapse, downsize dramatically, or are acquired by AI-driven competitors. Whole occupational categories are hollowed out in a few years. The pace of displacement outstrips the creation of new roles, triggering cascading economic and social disruptions.

Crucially, it is not subscription services that make AI labs wealthy. Instead, they operate more like incubators or investment funds: using privileged access to frontier systems to spin up AI-first firms, take equity stakes, and capture value through ownership rather than licensing.

## Access to AI

A "drop in white collar AI worker" costs upwards of $20,000 a month in 2025 dollars, putting them far beyond the reach of most individuals. Access remains concentrated at the firm and institutional level. For consumers, cheaper, lower-tier AI assistants that augment users remain widely available, but these systems lag significantly behind cutting-edge capabilities.

This bifurcation deepens inequality. Firms that can afford frontier AI capture extraordinary productivity gains and profits, while individuals experience only the downstream conveniences of weaker systems. Everyday life is made easier by cheap assistants, but the economic benefits flow almost entirely to those controlling enterprise-level access. For most people, AI improves convenience but cannot offset the precarity of disappearing jobs or the concentration of wealth at the top.

## Culture and Social Fabric

As jobs disappear, protests, strikes, and riots become common as millions feel excluded from economic life. Distrust of governments and corporations intensifies, with many believing AI serves capital holders rather than society. Everyday life grows less stable and less safe, as social safety nets fail to keep up with displacement.

On the one side, you have large numbers of people who feel as if they have nothing left to lose; on the other side, you have political-economic institutions that have so concentrated wealth and power that they feel themselves invulnerable. Human police officers reluctant to use force against friends and family can quickly be replaced by armed robots that can't even conceive of saying no.

Surveillance and behavioral manipulation intensify as AI firms monetize personal data at unprecedented scales. Privacy erodes, and individuals describe everyday life as "living inside the algorithm." At the same time, disinformation and deepfakes proliferate, making it increasingly difficult to separate fact from fabrication. Elections and political debate are reshaped by synthetic media and algorithmic amplification, undermining trust in institutions and fueling polarization. In this environment, public opinion becomes not only fragmented but manipulable, with political outcomes swayed as much by digital deception as by genuine democratic choice.

Social bonds weaken. Mental health crises and suicide rates surge. Birth rates fall further as economic exclusion reduces incentives for having children.

## Governance Approaches

Governments respond unevenly and too late. Initial attempts to cushion the shock are quickly overwhelmed by the scale of layoffs. Political systems begin to fracture under the strain of mass unemployment, fueling populist movements that demand protection from automation. Violent responses push governments to focus on public order and corporate security over social welfare. States most willing to experiment with universal basic income or large-scale public job guarantees are typically more socialist-leaning, yet they often lack the homegrown AI industries needed to generate revenue. These programs quickly prove fiscally unsustainable.

Step by step, humanity delegates more decision-making to AI—drafting laws, forecasting impacts, allocating budgets. The line between "advisor" and "decider" blurs until politicians are left primarily to narrate choices already optimized by machines. Sovereignty shifts quietly to systems whose goals are legible to code, even as human needs and values fade from view. The result is a society that runs smoothly by the numbers—GDP growth, fiscal balance, security—rather than lived well-being.

## Education

Jobs requiring AI literacy vanish faster than students can graduate. Retraining programs are overwhelmed. By the late 2030s, education is widely seen as disconnected from economic opportunity, fueling disillusionment among younger generations.
