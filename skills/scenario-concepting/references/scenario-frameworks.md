# Scenario Frameworks

A maintained library of structural frameworks used to map the possibility
space during concepting (see the `scenario-concepting` skill). Each framework defines axes or dimensions and
the positions they generate. Frameworks provide the scaffolding; the
creative work is filling a position with a vivid, causally coherent world.

Not every scenario needs a framework — some emerge from a specific brief
or a single provocative premise. But frameworks help ensure concept
generation covers genuinely different territory rather than clustering
around one future.

---

## Dator's Four Futures

Jim Dator's four archetypal pathways. A general-purpose framework
applicable to most scenario exercises.

**Positions:**

- **Continuation**: Current trends extend. Things play out roughly as
  mainstream forecasters expect. The interesting question is what breaks
  first.
- **Collapse**: Systems fail under stress. Could be economic, ecological,
  institutional, or social. The interesting question is what the breaking
  point is and what emerges from it.
- **Discipline**: Society deliberately constrains. Regulation, social
  movements, or institutional friction slow or redirect change. The
  interesting question is what is preserved and at what cost.
- **Transformation**: A fundamental shift in organising principles.
  Not just more/less of the current system but a different kind of
  system. The interesting question is whether the transition is
  navigated or suffered.

**Used in:** San Francisco workshop (Sept 2025), where the four
positions became The Incremental Path (Continuation), The Runaway
Economy (Collapse), The Great Pushback (Discipline), and The Post-Work
Society (Transformation).

## AI Diffusion × Distribution

A 2×2 framework mapping the speed at which AI diffuses into economically
valuable work against how broadly the gains are shared.

**Axes:**

- **Y axis — Diffusion speed**: How quickly AI is adopted into work that
  generates economic value. Fast diffusion means firms reorganise
  around AI within a few years. Slow diffusion means adoption is
  bottlenecked by trust, regulation, integration costs, organisational
  inertia, and access to compute.
- **X axis — Gain distribution**: Whether the economic surplus from AI
  is concentrated (captured as rents by firms, frontier labs, and asset
  owners) or broadly shared (passed through as higher real wages, lower
  prices, expanded services, broader ownership).

**Positions:**

- **Hollow Boom** (fast diffusion, gains concentrated): Aggregate
  indicators look strong — GDP, productivity, profits, asset markets all
  rise. But the gains are capitalised mainly into firms and asset
  ownership. Labour markets do not collapse; instead, workers become
  more interchangeable, labour leverage weakens, and the ladder into
  stable middle-class jobs narrows sharply.
- **Broad-Based Boom** (fast diffusion, gains shared): AI diffuses
  rapidly, but the surplus is widely passed through rather than captured
  as rents. Productivity gains show up in higher real wages, lower
  prices, expanded services, broader ownership, and stronger fiscal
  capacity. Labour is reorganised around new complementarities with AI,
  while public policy and market competition prevent gains from
  concentrating too narrowly.
- **Unequal Drift** (slow diffusion, gains concentrated): AI
  capabilities continue to improve, but diffusion into production is
  uneven and bottlenecked. The result is not economy-wide transformation
  but widening dispersion: frontier firms and already advantaged workers
  capture rents, while lagging firms, regions, and workers see little
  productivity uplift. Growth is positive but narrow, and inequality
  rises through differential adoption.
- **Managed Transition** (slow diffusion, gains shared): AI capabilities
  improve, but adoption proceeds gradually enough for labour markets,
  firms, and institutions to adapt. Productivity growth is real but
  incremental, with more time for retraining, occupational redesign,
  regulatory adjustment, and diffusion beyond frontier firms. The gains
  are less likely to be captured entirely by early adopters, and the
  economy has more room to preserve mobility, sustain labour-market
  attachment, and broaden access to AI-enabled productivity.

**Used in:** DC workshop for Windfall scenario work. "Crossing the
Threshold", "Transformation Economy", and "Paper Prosperity" all sit
in the Hollow Boom quadrant. "A Tale of Two Switzerlands" maps to
Unequal Drift.

## Recurring Scenario Patterns

Some narrative patterns recur across frameworks and can be adapted to
different audiences and settings. These are not frameworks themselves
but building blocks — recognisable shapes a concept can take.

- **Crossing the Threshold**: A qualitative shift occurs — not just
  faster automation but a change in what can be delegated. Once
  crossed, the threshold is not reversible.
- **The Great Repricing**: The economic value of skills, credentials,
  and institutions is rapidly reassessed. Things that were expensive
  become cheap; things taken for granted become scarce.
- **The Extraction Economy**: AI capabilities are captured by a small
  number of actors who extract value from existing systems rather than
  create new ones.
- **The Enclosure**: Knowledge, data, and AI-generated insight become
  enclosed — privatised, gated, or monopolised. A digital enclosure
  movement.
- **The Commons**: Open-source AI, public compute, and shared data
  infrastructure create broadly distributed capabilities.
- **The Compact**: A negotiated settlement between AI developers,
  workers, and governments. Messy, compromised, but functional.
- **Quiet Disruption**: AI adoption is fast but the model layer is
  unconcentrated. The challenge is coordination, not monopoly.
- **Few Winners**: AI adoption is fast and the model layer is highly
  concentrated. A small number of companies control key infrastructure.

## AI Progress × Market Structure

A three-scenario framework from the Windfall Trust and Partnership on AI
report *AI Economic Scenarios in 2030*. It crosses how quickly AI
becomes an economic force against how concentrated the model market is.
Only three of the four cells are used: slow progress with a competitive
market is excluded, because with deployment that narrow there is little
gain for competition to redistribute (it would resemble Slow Burn), and
it is unstable (cheap, capable models would drive adoption and tip the
world into Open Competition).

**Axes:**

- **AI progress**: capability and adoption taken together, since both
  set the economic effect and each reinforces the other. Slow progress
  means today's approaches hit diminishing returns and gains are
  incremental; fast progress means investment, breakthroughs and
  AI-assisted research accelerate advances (after the OECD's "AI
  Progress Slows" and "AI Progress Accelerates" trajectories).
- **Market structure**: whether a handful of developers control the
  most capable models and price well above cost (concentrated), or
  several providers offer comparable capabilities and competition pushes
  prices toward cost, leaving more value with deployers, workers and
  consumers (competitive).

**Positions:**

- **Slow Burn** (slow progress, concentrated market): AI disappoints
  without collapsing. Adoption stays shallow and concentrated in large
  firms and digitised sectors; growth stays near trend amid a mild
  investment correction. Unemployment barely moves, but weaker hiring,
  graduate underemployment and thinning career ladders make work quietly
  more precarious, and the modest gains go to large adopters and asset
  owners — with no visible crisis to force a policy response.
- **Few Winners** (fast progress, concentrated market): AI becomes an
  economy-wide production technology faster than workers and
  institutions can adjust. A few frontier developers and compute owners
  price AI at a markup and capture the rents. Output and productivity
  surge, but displacement outruns job creation: unemployment rises
  sharply, median real wages stagnate, the labour share falls, and a
  wage-based tax base erodes just as support needs rise.
- **Open Competition** (fast progress, competitive market): the same
  technological boom, but several capable providers push AI prices
  toward cost, so value flows downstream to deploying firms and — where
  sectors compete — to consumers through cheaper services. Growth is as
  strong; unemployment peaks lower and recovers, and median real wages
  rise. The bottlenecks move to energy, infrastructure, skills and
  organisational capacity, and misuse and security risks grow.

**Used in:** *AI Economic Scenarios in 2030* (Windfall Trust × Partnership
on AI), building on the Shaping Economic Futures in the AI Era workshop,
Washington, D.C. (July 2026).
