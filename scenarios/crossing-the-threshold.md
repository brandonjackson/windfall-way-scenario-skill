# UK 2030: Crossing the Threshold

In its early years, AI behaved like a normal technology. Fears of rogue superintelligences never materialized. Instead investments into responsible AI paid off. AI systems were increasingly thought of as humble and competent, on a level with dentists. Adoption was slow. But policymakers were cautiously optimistic about its potential to drive growth. Then, on or about December 2027, the economy's character changed. It was not that one went out, and found robots walking down the street. The change was not sudden and definite like that. But a change there was, nevertheless. AI had finally become "good enough" to enable substitution. And what started in data centres soon diffused through offices worldwide. By October 2030, nearly a third of earners in the top 20% had either lost their jobs or seen their roles dramatically downgraded.

## Capabilities

By December 2027 frontier models were capable of performing 4 hour cognitive tasks without human supervision: writing legal briefs, building full financial models for business deals, designing and implementing software features end to end. Every year the length of tasks they could perform autonomously roughly doubled. Models couldn't do everything. But hundreds of foreign startups leveraged frontier AI to close the last mile, building a new generation of killer apps targeting the high-skill, high-wage jobs that had powered 20th century social mobility.

## Rising Underemployment

In the mid-2020s AI began destroying entry-level white collar jobs. Yet for most older voters the fading economic possibilities of their grandchildren were easy to ignore. Everything changed as AI worked its way up the pyramid. Apps began automating the most complex parts of the professions like software engineering, law, accounting, and finance. Large firms began to make deep cuts. Soon the middle rungs of the career ladder had vanished. It was unclear where it would stop. The media was filled with interviews of AI firm CEOs smiling saying "And just imagine - this is the worst these systems will ever be." New jobs were still being created. But they weren't good jobs. They were lower paid, less secure, with fewer ladders. People who had followed the rules, earned degrees and built careers now found themselves competing for gig work with little hope of their old jobs coming back. Abundant intelligence had permanently devalued expertise.

## Hollow Growth

The wave of job losses triggered a wave of windfall investment returns in British corporations. These gains powered GDP growth crescendoing in Q4 2028 at 4%. However, this growth started slowing due to a drop in consumer spending. Many factors were at play. An increasing amount of economic activity didn't show up in GDP since it was transacted for the near-free price of inference. But more worrying still: the high earners being displaced were also the consumers who powered the economy. And their digital replacements didn't eat out, go shopping (or pay taxes). This sharp drop in demand was increasingly felt on high streets around the country. By 2030 many retail and hospitality firms struggled to see a way forward.

## Fiscal Crisis

In the end the government could not afford to manage the transition. Multinational profits and asset values were rising—but in the UK it is mostly labour and consumption that are taxed, not capital. Historically the top 20% of earners had paid 75% of all income tax. OBR forecasts showed falling tax receipts and rising bills for a straining social safety net, triggering fiscal black holes year after year, each one larger than the last.

## Broken Promises

The same people who predicted AI would lead to abundance confidently predicted new jobs would come. The wealthiest enjoyed the falling price of financial and legal services. Meanwhile, everyone else watched as the cost of living continued to rise. Powerful forces began to demand urgent change. Unrest arose amongst the newly underemployed. And the bond markets began to express their view: that the UK needed to cut public spending to adjust to its new reality: being highly automated, asset rich, and fiscally poor.
