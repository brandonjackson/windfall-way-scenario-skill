# The Post-Work Society

> Scenario 4 from the Windfall Trust "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI" workshop, September 2025, San Francisco. Created by Anna Yelizarova, Danny Buerkli, Jamais Cascio, and David Evan Harris.

AI replaces most human labor, but rather than driving exclusion, societies harness its productivity to expand leisure and improve quality of life. Effective, coordinated global redistribution ensures that the gains are widely shared, giving people the freedom to pursue education, creativity, care work, and community. Reformed institutions provide not only economic security but also new avenues for meaning and fulfillment, transforming the disruption of automation into an opportunity for human flourishing.

## State of AI Tech

General-purpose AI systems achieve the long-anticipated breakthrough: they can perform nearly all forms of cognitive and creative labor at or above human level. From design to law, logistics to medicine, execution and decision-making are increasingly handled by machines. Large firms reorganize around fully automated workflows, and small firms scale effortlessly with minimal staff. The economic rationale for employing humans in most traditional roles disappears.

Yet this does not result in collapse. Instead, governments and societies respond by embracing a transition away from work as the central organizing principle of economic and social life. AI delivers unprecedented productivity and abundance, freeing people from the necessity of traditional jobs. Human labor remains in niches where empathy, embodiment, and creativity are valued, but for most people, work becomes optional rather than compulsory.

## Access to AI

Advanced AI capabilities become public goods through universal basic assets and public infrastructure. While individuals can choose their level of AI assistance, the default is universal access rather than market-based exclusion.

## Culture and Social Fabric

The transition to post-work society requires profound cultural transformation, dismantling centuries-old assumptions that tie human worth to employment. The transition is not without challenges. Initially, some struggle with purposelessness, identity crises, and overreliance on AI companions and digital amusements that lead some to retreat from civic life.

Gradually, new frameworks emerge. Art, community service, caregiving, and cultural preservation become recognized contributions. Extended families and chosen communities replace workplace relationships as primary social structures. Birth rates stabilize as economic pressures disappear, though family structures diversify significantly. By 2050, younger generations create vibrant cultures around creativity and community stewardship, though significant portions of older populations never fully adjust to the post-work paradigm.

Ultimately, the social contract is rebuilt around dignity without labor: society affirms that to be human is valuable in itself. But reaching this equilibrium requires a generational cultural adjustment, experimentation with new institutions, and painful transition periods where old identities crumble faster than new ones cohere.

## Governance Approaches

The technology develops rapidly but within carefully designed institutional frameworks that ensure broad benefit distribution. Rather than market forces alone determining deployment, democratic processes and international coordination guide AI implementation.

High taxation of AI-driven profits and capital funds universal basic income schemes, expanded universal services, and cultural programs. Policy experiments once seen as utopian like global dividend funds become mainstream. Instead of attempting to preserve jobs, regulators focus on ensuring fair distribution of AI's gains and maintaining social cohesion. Institutions adapt quickly, redefining their legitimacy around the promise of shared abundance rather than employment.

Governments reshape AI from private commodity to public utility. Just as universal healthcare and education are considered social rights, access to AI capabilities becomes a public service. Government-funded AI systems provide free tutoring, basic legal advice, healthcare diagnosis support, and small business assistance. This "Public AI" infrastructure ensures that the productivity benefits don't exclusively flow to those who can afford premium corporate AI subscriptions.

## Education

In the post-work society, education is no longer a pipeline into employment. People go to school, not to upskill for the labor market but to pursue knowledge, creativity, and self-discovery in communities of peers. Universities and learning centers become civic institutions where people of all ages gather to study philosophy, art, science, and ethics alongside AI tutors. Education becomes a lifelong public good, a place to explore what it means to be human in an age when work is no longer the measure of value.
